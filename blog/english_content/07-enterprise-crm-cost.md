Enterprise CRM cost is driven by the depth of workflows, permissions, integrations, data migration and operational requirements. A contact database and a multi-branch service platform are not comparable purchases. Before comparing prices, define the system and the work required to make it usable.

This international edition uses US dollars. References were reviewed on October 12, 2026. The figures are external commercial benchmarks or clearly labelled planning calculations, not Rvion prices, guaranteed quotations or conversions from Iranian currency.

## What the international figures actually mean

[Syndicode's CRM development guide](https://syndicode.com/blog/how-to-build-a-crm/) estimates $50,000–$300,000 for custom CRM development. Its example timeline is 3–4 months for an MVP and 6–12 months or longer for a fuller system. It is a development vendor, not a neutral statistical source; its scope may differ substantially from yours. A complex enterprise rollout can fall outside that band.

For an alternative bottom-up illustration, 2,000–6,000 team hours at an assumed blended $50–$100 per hour produces $100,000–$600,000. These hours and rates are our scenario assumptions, not a market finding. They demonstrate why a quote changes with scope, team and location, not what every enterprise must spend. The top-down vendor range and this calculation are different ways to frame uncertainty, not amounts to add together.

Configuration of an existing platform is another category altogether. Do not apply a from-scratch development budget to a product installation or assume an installation price covers a new application.

## Five scope drivers

### Workflows, modules and reporting

Leads, opportunities and customer profiles are familiar concepts. Costs rise when assignment rules, multi-stage approvals, service deadlines and custom reports become deeply interconnected. Define the operational decisions each dashboard must support. A large collection of charts is not a substitute for an actionable work queue.

### Roles and permissions

Specify who can view, edit, export or delete each kind of information. Field-level restrictions, team boundaries and delegated responsibilities require design and negative tests. Make the permission matrix part of acceptance rather than an informal request after launch.

### Integrations

Document the systems, API quality, synchronisation direction, volumes and failure behaviour. A connector is not complete merely because one successful request works. Plan retries, duplicate prevention, audit trails and reconciliation. Include responsibility when a supplier changes an API or becomes unavailable.

### Data migration

Audit the source data before fixing a price. Identify duplicates, obsolete records, mandatory mappings and sensitive fields. Rehearse the import, reconcile counts and sample relationships, and define how the cutover can be reversed without losing new work. Moving a large, inconsistent database is not equivalent to importing a clean contact list.

### Deployment and security

Choose the deployment model before estimating. Requirements for separate infrastructure, backup retention, audit logging, monitoring and recovery affect architecture. Self-hosting provides control, not automatic safety. Someone must own patching, incident response and restoration tests.

## Compare subscription costs fairly

[Salesforce's current public sales pricing](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/pricing/) lists paid editions across $25–$550 per user per month. Feature sets, commitments and add-ons vary. For an illustrative 50-user team comparing $25–$100 monthly tiers, annual base licences are $15,000–$60,000. This excludes implementation, migration, extras and internal staff time. Verify the actual plan, billing terms, eligibility and regional restrictions directly with the supplier.

Do not assume custom development becomes cheaper after a particular number of months. Compare three-to-five-year scenarios with user growth, implementation, operating costs, upgrades and exit costs. Ready-made products may remain the better choice even for a large team if the fit is strong.

## Delivery stages and acceptance

1. **Discovery:** interview process owners and frontline staff; produce a prioritised scope and measurable outcomes.
2. **Design:** validate the data model, permission matrix and clickable operational flows.
3. **Incremental implementation:** deliver a usable slice and test integrations under failure, not only success.
4. **Migration rehearsal:** clean, map, import and reconcile before the real cutover.
5. **User acceptance:** let representative staff perform complete daily tasks.
6. **Training and rollout:** train by role and support a pilot before wider release.
7. **Operation:** review incidents, adoption, response times and improvement requests.

Keep time estimates attached to their assumptions. A product configuration schedule cannot be reused as a custom-build promise. Assign an internal owner with authority to resolve conflicting requirements.

## Hidden costs and a support budget

Include internal workshops, data cleanup, training, documentation, infrastructure and third-party licences. For maintenance, an illustrative 20–60 hours monthly at $50–$100 per hour gives $1,000–$6,000 per month, or $12,000–$72,000 annually. This is our workload calculation, not a universal percentage or vendor guarantee; hosting, round-the-clock cover and major feature work may be additional.

Define service hours, incident priorities, response expectations and what qualifies as a change request. [Ongoing maintenance](/en/services/maintenance-and-growth/) is part of the product's life, not an optional decoration after delivery.

## Contract and proposal checks

Fixed pricing is most useful after scope is understood. Time-and-materials work needs regular budget review and visible deliverables. A discovery phase can reduce uncertainty before either model is chosen. Clarify intellectual-property rights, third-party licences, handover and support in the written agreement; obtain appropriate professional advice on the contract.

Watch for a single unexplained development total, no migration plan, no testing budget, unclear ownership and a deadline without dependencies. A low quote may simply describe less work.

- Document workflows, modules and priorities.
- Audit data and identify what will not migrate.
- Specify access, integrations and deployment requirements.
- Compare the same ownership period and cost assumptions.
- Require acceptance, training, recovery and handover evidence.
