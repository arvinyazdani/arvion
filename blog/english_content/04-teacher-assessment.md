An English teacher's language accuracy and teaching ability are related but different. A fluent conversation does not establish that a candidate can explain an error, select an appropriate exercise or manage a lesson. A defensible hiring process combines structured screening, an interview and a teaching demonstration.

This guide does not offer an accredited qualification or claim that an online score can determine teaching competence on its own.

## What an interview can and cannot show

Interviews reveal motivation, communication and professional judgement. Their value improves when candidates receive comparable questions and interviewers use an agreed rubric. An informal conversation can overvalue confidence or familiarity and miss weaknesses in written accuracy or instructional reasoning.

Accent should not be confused with competence. Ask whether speech is intelligible and appropriate for the role, not whether the candidate resembles an imagined native-speaker ideal. [Jack Richards' discussion of language-teacher competence](https://jalt-publications.org/sites/default/files/pdf-article/plen1.pdf) distinguishes the language resources needed for teaching from a requirement for native-like mastery.

Use the interview to investigate evidence, including examples of lesson planning, feedback and adapting an explanation. Do not eliminate it simply because an automated screening stage is available.

## Separate language accuracy from teaching analysis

### Language accuracy

A practical screening framework can examine grammar in context, precise vocabulary, critical reading, listening and editing. Tasks should distinguish plausible interpretations and require attention to meaning, not reward memorised rules alone. These areas are a proposed hiring framework, not a universal certification standard.

For example, ask a candidate to explain why a learner's sentence is unsuitable in a particular context, then describe a better alternative. Knowing a rule and explaining it clearly are different kinds of evidence.

### Instructional reasoning

Present a classroom scenario: a learner repeatedly makes the same error despite correction. Ask for possible causes, the next feedback step and a short practice activity. Avoid treating one preferred teaching method as the only valid response. The evaluation rubric should reward reasoning, suitability for the learner and the ability to check understanding.

An advanced language score alone does not measure lesson planning, classroom management, work with children or specialist exam coaching. Add role-specific tasks when those skills matter.

## Qualifications, screening and demonstrations

A degree, a recent proficiency result and a teaching qualification answer different questions. Verify what each credential actually represents. An internal screening test adds value by giving applicants a comparable task and helping identify topics to explore later; it does not automatically inherit the validity or recognition of an established examination.

A teaching demonstration shows classroom presence and pacing, but one short lesson is still a limited sample. Use a common brief and scoring form, and preferably more than one assessor. Combine it with references and observation during a probationary period.

## Integrity monitoring provides signals, not certainty

Remote assessment can involve outside assistance, search or AI tools. Browser monitoring cannot prove that no such assistance occurred, and an isolated event is not proof of cheating. A candidate may encounter an accessibility tool, technical fault or interrupted connection.

- Explain the assessment rules before starting.
- Review the answers and context, not just a risk label.
- Distinguish a copy attempt from successfully obtaining or using outside help.
- Interpret unusual answer times alongside question difficulty and response quality.
- Give candidates a route to report technical problems or challenge a decision.

Automated rules can restrict continuation under a disclosed policy, but personnel decisions require care. In an interview, ask applicants to explain one or two of their own responses. Use that conversation to understand their reasoning, not to treat hesitation as a confession.

## Read a result as a profile

Candidates with the same total score may have different strengths. Where a report provides skill-level detail, examine it and identify what the role requires. Set minimum expectations before reviewing names or results, then consider borderline cases consistently.

Do not present a locally produced certificate or CEFR-aligned estimate as an official internationally accredited credential without evidence. Check what the assessment actually issues and how any level estimate was established. Classroom performance remains a separate question.

## Budget for the whole selection process

There is no universal global price for teacher screening. Use the assessment provider's current checkout price and separately budget staff time. As a planning method, multiply the expected assessor hours by your own USD hourly cost to create a low-to-high range, then add any per-candidate fees. This article does not invent a USD selling price for Rvion's locally billed assessment.

For a school hiring only a few teachers, a structured interview and demonstration with verified credentials may be sufficient. A separate screening product is useful only if someone reviews its output and acts on it.

## Fairness and candidate experience

[ILTA's practice guidelines](https://www.iltaonline.com/page/ILTAGuidelinesforPractice2024) provide a reference point for responsible language assessment. Translate that principle into clear instructions, comparable conditions, appropriate accommodations and an explanation of who can see results. Avoid promises that monitoring is infallible.

- Define the role and the language and teaching evidence required.
- Use fixed interview questions with a scoring rubric.
- Give shortlisted candidates a comparable demonstration brief.
- Review integrity events in context and retain a technical-problem route.
- Explain how the result will be used before the candidate starts.
- Verify employment history and references separately.
