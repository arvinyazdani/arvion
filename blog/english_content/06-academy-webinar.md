A good academy website follows a simple journey: understand a course, check its suitability and enrol with clear expectations. Instructor biographies, webinars and memberships should support that journey rather than compete with it. Decide whether the primary goal is attracting new students or supporting existing ones before drawing the navigation.

## Separate prospective and current students

New visitors need outcomes, prerequisites, dates, prices and refund conditions. Current students need schedules, recordings, access instructions and help. Use public course pages for discovery and a separate student area where ongoing access is needed. A single overloaded homepage serves neither audience well.

## A practical site structure

- **Homepage:** explain what is taught and for whom, with clear course and webinar routes.
- **Course catalogue:** concise cards linking to an individual page for each course.
- **Instructors:** genuine biographies with relevant experience and links to their courses.
- **Webinars:** upcoming events and useful replay pages.
- **Enrolment and policies:** payment, attendance, certificate and refund information.
- **Help and contact:** answers to real recurring questions.

Make the links normal HTML links so the journey remains accessible and discoverable. The [academy demonstration](/en/projects/demos/ariana-academy/) is an example to explore, not a live course provider.

## Anatomy of a course page

Begin with an accurate title and a concrete learning outcome. Follow it with the intended audience and prerequisites, including who the course is not for. Publish the syllabus as readable page content rather than only a downloadable document.

State whether teaching is live, recorded or in person; identify the schedule, time zone, session length and instructor. Show the final price and what it includes before the main enrolment action. Place refund and absence conditions where people make the decision, not behind an obscure footer link.

Repeat one primary action at useful points instead of offering numerous equally prominent buttons. Use real questions in the FAQ, such as whether a missed class has a recording. Do not promise guaranteed employment or official accreditation without the necessary evidence.

## Keep enrolment predictable

A useful flow is course selection, a short form, payment or a request, and a confirmation that explains the next step. Collect only required information and handle failed payments, duplicate clicks and interrupted sessions. Where accounts are necessary for access, explain why rather than surprising users at checkout.

Confirmation should identify the course, schedule and access route. Clarify the communication channel and provide a support alternative when an email or text does not arrive. Corporate or placement-dependent courses may need consultation rather than immediate payment.

## A webinar page has two lives

Before the event, show the topic, speakers, date, time zone, outline and replay policy. Provide calendar information and a short registration process. Afterwards, keep the same URL and replace registration emphasis with the recording, a summary, resources and a related course.

For recordings, create a useful page rather than a bare embedded player. If access is restricted, separate public descriptive content from the protected recording. Read [Google's video guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video) before assuming a video or its metadata qualifies for a particular search feature.

## Add membership only when there is a continuing offer

A learning path can begin as a simple page describing the sequence and prerequisites of several courses. Membership is an operational commitment: regular content, recurring billing, access control, cancellation and support. If the offer is a few independent courses, validate demand before adding that complexity.

## Build trust with evidence

Use genuine instructor identities, accurate experience and student feedback published with permission. Say what a certificate represents. Avoid fictional testimonials, invented enrolment counts and unverified job-placement promises. A clear syllabus and honest policy are more useful than decorative trust badges.

## SEO without unnecessary complexity

Give each course a stable URL, a distinctive title and useful descriptive content. Link from the catalogue to courses and from each webinar to relevant learning options. Consolidate duplicate URLs and keep private account and checkout pages out of search results using the appropriate indexing controls.

[Google's SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) is a practical reference. Helpful page content matters more than adding structured-data types solely in the hope of a rich result. Search features and eligibility can change; do not make guaranteed visibility part of the sales promise.

Measure course-page visits, enrolment starts and completions, and where people stop. For webinars, examine registration, attendance, replay use and movement to a course. Use privacy-appropriate measurement and connect findings to a decision rather than collecting data without a purpose.

## Start with the simplest workable system

A few courses with reception-led enrolment may need only an informative [business website](/en/services/corporate-website-design/). Consider a [custom application](/en/services/custom-web-application/) when prerequisites, capacity, payments and access rules cannot be managed reliably by existing tools.

- Give every course a clear page and action.
- Show the full price and conditions early.
- Preserve webinar URLs after the event.
- Test confirmation, access and support on mobile.
- Budget for content production and operation, not just launch.
