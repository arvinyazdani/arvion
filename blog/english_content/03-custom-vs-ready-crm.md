A ready-made CRM is usually the better starting point for a small team with a standard sales process and an urgent launch. Customisation or a custom build becomes worth investigating when important work cannot fit the product, integrations are missing or available deployment options cannot meet your data requirements.

The decision is about fit and responsibility, not simply subscription price. The USD references were reviewed on October 16, 2026; they are not Rvion quotations.

## What a CRM should connect

A useful CRM brings leads, sales opportunities, customer records, communications, service requests and management reporting into one operational picture. The difficult choices often appear at the edges: a special approval rule, a legacy system or sensitive information that must not be visible to every employee.

Rvion's [CRM overview](/en/crm/) describes its customer, sales, service and operations offering. Compare any product against your actual processes rather than a feature checklist alone.

## There are four options, not two

- **Cloud SaaS:** subscribe to a product operated by its vendor.
- **Self-hosted ready-made software:** deploy an established product on infrastructure you control.
- **A customised product:** adapt an existing core to your organisation.
- **A new custom build:** implement a system around your requirements.

Self-hosting and custom development are separate decisions. Do not commission new software solely because you need a separate database; investigate suitable self-hosted products first.

## Eight practical decision criteria

### 1. Workflow fit

Write your three most important workflows step by step and perform them in a trial. Record every spreadsheet, manual workaround and duplicate entry. A missing decorative dashboard is less important than an approval that cannot be enforced.

### 2. Data control

Clarify storage location, exports, backups, retention and privileged access. A separate deployment provides control but also transfers operational responsibility. Ask a qualified adviser about the rules applicable to your organisation; control is not automatically compliance.

### 3. Integration

List accounting, messaging, telephony, website and inventory systems. For each, identify a supported connector, a documented API or a new development requirement. Specify synchronisation direction, error handling, reconciliation and ownership when an external system changes.

### 4. Users and ownership cost

[Salesforce's official Sales pricing page](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/pricing/) lists paid editions from Starter Suite at $25 per user/month through Max at $550 per user/month; the intervening listed tiers are Pro Suite ($100), Core ($195) and Advanced ($395). The price range is unchanged in this review, though plan names now include Core and Max. Billing commitments, features, add-ons and regional availability differ. For illustration, 20 users at $25–$100 monthly means $6,000–$24,000 yearly for licences alone; this is arithmetic, not a vendor quotation for your team.

For a new custom build, use one consistent planning reference: our [custom software cost breakdown](/en/blog/custom-software-cost-us-2026-crm-accounting-b2b/) aligns with the [cost calculator](/en/tools/cost-calculator/): basic CRM $24,000–$50,000, standard $54,000–$112,000 and enterprise $150,000–$360,000+. The basic upper bound is rounded from GMWARE's published $24,000–$45,000 estimate to allow for scope variation; these are planning bands, not a quote. [Syndicode's guide](https://syndicode.com/blog/how-to-build-a-crm/) is a vendor guide that currently gives $30,000–$100,000 for a basic CRM and over $150,000 for advanced systems. Its scope and commercial assumptions differ, so do not combine its figures with the shared planning bands. Configuration of an existing product is a different scope and may cost much less. Compare licensing, implementation, migration, internal staff time, hosting, maintenance and exit costs over the same period.

### 5. Time to value

A trial product can be usable quickly; a custom system requires discovery, implementation and validation. The same Syndicode guide estimates 3–4 months for an MVP and 6–12 months or longer for a fuller build. These are its examples, not a promise for your organisation. Start with a focused operational slice when a large rollout would delay learning.

### 6. Adoption

Let real sales and service staff test the proposed screens. A system designed only around management's assumptions can be ignored even when every requested feature exists. Appoint an internal owner, train by role and run a pilot with clear success criteria.

### 7. Security

Ask who applies patches, reviews permissions, responds to incidents and proves that backups can be restored. In SaaS, some infrastructure duties sit with the vendor, but account security and configuration remain important. In a separate deployment, responsibilities must be explicitly assigned to your organisation or technical partner.

### 8. Maintenance and dependence

SaaS creates dependence on vendor pricing and product direction; custom work creates dependence on code knowledge and maintenance capacity. Document exports, handover, support commitments and code rights. Include [continuing maintenance](/en/services/maintenance-and-growth/) in the financial model.

## When ready-made is the right answer

Choose a trial first when the sales process is conventional, requirements are evolving, integrations already exist and nobody can own a custom product. Availability and permitted use still matter: verify the supplier's current regional and contractual restrictions directly. An English-language price page is not proof that every organisation can buy the service.

## When custom work belongs on the shortlist

Investigate it when essential workflows remain outside the system, a critical integration cannot be supported, or suitable ready-made deployments fail explicit data requirements. A long usage horizon and many users may improve the economics, but there is no automatic break-even month. Compare a three-to-five-year model using your own assumptions.

## Reduce risk before committing

Run a limited pilot or commission discovery before a large build. Require standard data exports from the start. If the requirement extends beyond CRM, consider a broader [custom application](/en/services/custom-web-application/) rather than forcing unrelated operations into sales software.

- Document processes, integrations and access rules.
- Nominate an internal product owner and pilot team.
- Compare the same ownership period and user-growth assumptions.
- Specify recovery, support and an exit plan.
- Measure whether staff can complete real work without parallel spreadsheets.
