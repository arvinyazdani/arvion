A US-based freelance Django developer typically charges about $67–$104 per hour at junior level, $85–$135 mid-level and $110–$171 senior in 2026, according to one modeled benchmark. Upwork's Django-specific cost page instead reports $20–$40 per hour from historical contracts worldwide; it is not a US-only or seniority-specific benchmark. These are published estimates, not quotes.

## Django freelancer rates in the US (2026)

One 2026 benchmark breaks hourly cost down by engagement model (USD):

| Model | Junior (1–3 yrs) | Mid (3–6 yrs) | Senior (7–10 yrs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freelance / contract | $67–$104 | $85–$135 | $110–$171 |
| In-house employee | $75–$115 | $95–$150 | $120–$190 |
| Agency / dev shop | $130–$215 | $165–$275 | $210–$350 |

That source is a vendor and says its numbers are modeled, not sampled. Employee salaries and contractor prices are different measures; do not convert an annual salary into a freelance quote without accounting for non-billable time and overhead.

The [Upwork Django cost guide](https://www.upwork.com/hire/django-developers/cost/) gives a worldwide historical range, not a US rate by experience. Compare it with the [Second Talent US model](https://www.secondtalent.com/cost-to-hire/django-developer/), which is vendor-published rather than a survey of actual freelance quotes.

## What you get at each level

- **Junior:** good for well-specified tasks under review; cheaper per hour, slower overall.
- **Mid-level:** can own a feature or a small app with some guidance.
- **Senior:** designs the data model and architecture, handles security, deployment and integrations, and reduces rework. Often the best value for a new product.

## Hourly or fixed price?

Use **hourly** when scope may change or you are exploring the product. Use **fixed price** when deliverables are clear: a defined list of screens, roles, integrations and acceptance tests. A common middle path is a paid discovery phase, then a fixed price for the first milestone.

## Scope the work so quotes are comparable

1. List features by user role and screen.
2. List integrations (payments, email, accounting, ERP) and the vendor APIs.
3. Define "done": acceptance criteria and a staging environment you can test.
4. Specify the stack (for example Python and Django, PostgreSQL) and hosting.
5. Ask for milestones with payments tied to working software.

## Contract points to settle early

- **Ownership:** the code and designs should be assigned to you on payment.
- **Access:** you should own the repository, cloud accounts and domain from day one.
- **Quality:** tests, documentation and a handover session.
- **Maintenance:** agree a retainer or a rate; yearly upkeep is commonly 10–20% of the build cost.
- **Communication:** weekly demo and a shared task board.

## Red flags

A very low bid with no questions about your scope, no plan for testing or deployment, refusal to share the repository, or promises of a complex product in a few days.

## FAQ

**How much does a Django developer cost per hour in the US?** About $85–$135 for a mid-level freelancer and $110–$171 for a senior, per one 2026 benchmark; Upwork's Django-specific guide reports $20–$40 across historical worldwide contracts, not a US seniority range.

**Is it cheaper to hire a freelancer than an agency?** Not necessarily; compare like-for-like scope, review, support and delivery capacity rather than assuming a fixed saving. For small, well-defined projects a senior freelancer is usually the best value; large projects need a team.

**Django or WordPress for my project?** WordPress is faster for content sites and common stores. Django suits custom logic such as CRMs, accounting tools, bookings and B2B portals.
